# Scoring and data integrity audit — 2026-10-17

A read-only review of the scoring and publication path: whether stored points match what the engine computes, whether the live data is internally consistent, whether the live schema matches `supabase/schema.sql`, and whether picks, results and scores can be written or changed in ways the rules don't allow. Nothing in the app, schema or live data was changed. Every finding below was re-checked by at least one independent verifier, and their corrections are folded into the text.

**How it was checked**
- Read the scoring and publication path end to end: `weekly-score-rows.ts`, `scoring.ts`, `results.ts`, `episode-publication.ts`, `results-reveal.ts`, `results-draft.ts`, `score-history.ts` and `grand-finale-late.ts` in `src/lib`; the admin results actions and the relevant parts of `results-form.tsx`; the publication RPCs, lock functions and pick/settings RPCs in `schema.sql`; and `scripts/recompute-published-weeks.ts`. A gap pass then read the snapshot and commit RPCs in detail, `update_league_settings`, `update_scoring_categories`, `create_league`, `submit_prediction`, `submit_grand_finale_prediction`, every waiver function, `applyEpisodeSchedule` and `recomputeWeekScores`.
- **Recompute.** Dumped every scoring input as one JSON and ran a scratch script that imports `buildWeeklyScoreRows` and diffs stored and recomputed rows in both directions, per league, manager, week and module. Weeks 1–5 were run in three "this episode" modes: latest, first and each.
- **Score History parity.** Ran `buildScoreHistory` over the same dump for every member and compared it with stored totals.
- **Multi-night and reveal edge cases.** Pure engine runs on the live dump with in-memory edits only.
- **Invariants (bulk read-only SQL):** roster interval overlaps per couple and per slot; open slots and roster size; `total_score` = Σ `judge_scores` and `scaled_score` = average × 3 (live and draft); published episodes have dances and results, and `status` matches `results_published_at`; draft rows and releases on published episodes; duplicates, co-manager-keyed rows and non-member rows across every team table; total = Σ module × weight; scoring settings against calibration; prediction validity (other season, already eliminated, second pick rules); dances, participants and results after elimination; spoiler progress, waiver claims and late unlocks; and pick and bracket timestamps against `prediction_lock_at` and the Grand Finale deadline, reimplemented from their bodies, with git history for the rule in force at the time.
- **Schema drift.** Loaded the whole `schema.sql` into PGlite (only the `pgcrypto` and publication lines stripped) and ran the same catalog query there and on live: columns, types, defaults, RLS flags, constraints, indexes, functions (identity args, `SECURITY DEFINER`, config, md5 of the whitespace-stripped body), function and table ACLs via `aclexplode`, column ACLs, policies (qual/with_check hashes) and triggers. Also checked live publications, event triggers, `pg_default_acl`, owners, extensions, storage and replica identity.
- **Attack tests, PGlite only.** Full `schema.sql`, `SET ROLE authenticated` with `auth.uid()` read from a GUC: Anchor Week reopening brackets, numeric poisoning of the publish, an unrelated pick during a publish, Recast back-dating (FCFS and manual), and a coast-switch reopen. Plus a pure-function check of the reveal path with a NaN weight.
- **Existing tests.** Ran Vitest on scoring, score-history, episode-publication (including the PGlite DB test) and couples-leaderboard: 110 passed.
- **Live access.** No live writes, and no throwaway accounts were created. Every live query ran as `supabase_read_only_user` through the Management API read-only SQL endpoint, plus one read-only Management API `GET` (PostgREST config). The service-role client was not used. Every write-path and attack test ran against in-memory PGlite copies of `schema.sql`.
- All scratch scripts live outside the repo. `git diff HEAD` on `src`, `schema.sql` and `scripts` was empty, so line numbers are at HEAD.

**Limits**
- **Live PostgREST argument precision (DATA-1)** was not reproduced live, because storing a poisoned value, even briefly on a throwaway league, would itself block every publish. The live claim rests on function bodies matching `schema.sql` and on PostgREST passing JSON numbers (or strings) to `numeric` parameters, which is standard behavior but wasn't observed here.
- **DATA-1's reveal-path insert failure** on a NaN weight is from reading the code. Only the `total_points: null` result was executed.
- **Recast (DATA-6)** wasn't exercised live, since every league is `waiver_mode` 'locked'.
- **RLS read-scoping** (peer picks before lock, hidden drafts) was not re-attack-tested. That is the security audit's job, and live policy hashes match `schema.sql`.
- **DATA-8, weeks 4–5 (manager 5b873ac9):** the timestamps fit both an RPC call under a since-changed coast or hours setting and a direct SQL insert using `now()`. Without settings history they can't be told apart.
- This pass finished within its budget; it says nothing about how long the remaining audit areas will take.

Severity:
- **High:** a real user or attacker can break scoring, see spoilers/peer picks/unreleased scores early, write to data they shouldn't, lose data, or take the site down on a live night.
- **Medium:** wrong behavior or real risk on a common path, or a likely live-night failure with a workaround.
- **Low:** hardening, polish, maintainability with a concrete payoff.

## Findings

**DATA-1 · Any signed-up user can block official results publication for every league by saving a number JavaScript can't round-trip** — High
- **Where:** [schema.sql:3609](../supabase/schema.sql#L3609) (the snapshot compare in `commit_episode_publication`; the same line is at [apply-atomic-episode-publication.sql:66](../supabase/apply-atomic-episode-publication.sql#L66), the copy applied live); [episode-publication.ts:189](../src/lib/episode-publication.ts#L176-L191) (sends back the JS-parsed snapshot); the unvalidated setters [schema.sql:1404](../supabase/schema.sql#L1404) (`update_league_settings`) and [schema.sql:1459](../supabase/schema.sql#L1459) (`update_scoring_categories`); [settings/actions.ts:212](../src/app/leagues/[id]/settings/actions.ts#L212-L245); [results.ts:297](../src/lib/results.ts#L292-L299) (reveal path).
- **What's wrong:** `publishEpisodeAtomically` reads `episode_publication_snapshot`, parses it in JS, and sends the parsed object back as `p_snapshot`. `commit_episode_publication` compares it to a fresh snapshot with `is distinct from`. The snapshot holds every league's `leagues` and `scoring_settings` row ([schema.sql:3561](../supabase/schema.sql#L3561-L3562)), and nothing stops those `numeric` columns from holding values a JS double can't represent. Two ways in:
  - **Precision variant (the most reliable):** `update_league_settings` writes `p_prediction_lock_hours_before_air` with no range, scale or lock check, and no Season Clock check at all, so it works on any league at any point in the season. `update_scoring_categories` does the same for every weight and point value. Its only gate is the Season Clock, and on a brand-new league that deadline rolls forward (`effective_hard_deadline_week` advances a `not_started` league's deadline), so it stays editable mid-season. Any account can call `create_league` and become a commissioner. Storing `1.00000000000000000001` or `1e400` means the JSON round trip changes the value, and every publish then fails with "Results or scoring inputs changed. Refresh and try again."
  - **NaN variant:** a NaN weight or point value comes through `to_jsonb` as the string `"NaN"`. `computeWeeklyScores` multiplies by it, `total_points` becomes NaN, `JSON.stringify` turns that into `null`, and the commit fails on `weekly_manager_scores.total_points NOT NULL`. This needs one score row that week in the attacker's league, set before that league's lock. NaN in lock hours alone does not break publish: it round-trips as `"NaN"` and isn't used in scoring.
  - Neither the RPCs nor the server action validates these values, and the attacker doesn't need the app: sign-ups are open and auto-confirmed, and a direct PostgREST call to `/rest/v1/rpc/update_league_settings` with the value sent as a JSON string is cast to `numeric` losslessly.
- **Why it matters:** one throwaway account can stop every league's official publish, for every episode, until someone hand-edits that one row with the service key. The error tells the admin to refresh, which never helps, and nothing points to the poisoned row in an unrelated league. Eliminations, Curtain Call, Grand Finale, `couples.status` and draft cleanup can't post on a live night. Per-couple reveal doesn't use the snapshot compare, so the precision variant still lets judges' points post. The NaN variant also breaks reveal when the attacker's league has a roster row that week: `buildWeeklyScoreRows` returns `total_points: null` and the insert fails partway through the league loop, leaving earlier leagues recomputed and the failing league's week rows already deleted.
- **Fix:**
  - Compare on the server instead of round-tripping jsonb through JS: have the snapshot RPC return `md5(snapshot::text)` alongside the data and pass only that hash back, or recompute and compare inside the commit. Change both `schema.sql` and `apply-atomic-episode-publication.sql`. This is the key part: bounds alone would leave any future unbounded `numeric` column in the snapshot open to the same failure.
  - Validate in SQL, since direct `/rpc` calls skip the server action: finite, bounded, scale-limited checks in `update_league_settings` and `update_scoring_categories` (e.g. weight 0–100, points 0–1000, lock hours −24 to 168, at most 4 decimal places, not NaN or Infinity), backed by CHECK constraints or narrower `numeric(p,s)` types the way `late_factor numeric(3,2)` already is.
- **Evidence:** PGlite test with the full `schema.sql`: `create_league` and the settings RPCs called as `authenticated` with a throwaway attacker's `auth.uid()`, then the real `buildPublicationPlan` with supabase-js-style `JSON.parse`/`JSON.stringify` and `commit_episode_publication`. Control: published OK. Lock hours `1.00000000000000000001`: RPC accepted, then the commit failed with "Results or scoring inputs changed." Curtain Call weight `1.00000000000000000001` and weight `1e400`: same failure. Weight NaN plus one pick that week: not-null violation on `total_points`. A pure reveal-mode run of `buildWeeklyScoreRows` with weight `"NaN"` returned `total_points: null`. Live function bodies for these four functions match `schema.sql`, and a live read-only scan of every number in the snapshot tables found none that fail the double round trip and no NaN or Infinity, so live publish isn't poisoned today. **3/3 upheld.** All three reproduced the precision variant; two also confirmed the NaN variant works only through a weight or point value (NaN lock hours published fine), while the third verified only the precision and `1e400` variants and left NaN as plausible. Verifiers added the direct-`/rpc` route, the `apply-atomic-episode-publication.sql` copy and the partial-state note on the reveal path.

**DATA-2 · Late Grand Finale entries can earn points for an elimination that has aired but isn't published yet** — Medium
- **Where:** [schema.sql:3092](../supabase/schema.sql#L3090-L3098) (the `ineligible_couple_ids` snapshot in `submit_grand_finale_prediction`); [schema.sql:2961](../supabase/schema.sql#L2957-L2965) (the acknowledgement check in `unlock_grand_finale_late`); [weekly-score-rows.ts:143](../src/lib/weekly-score-rows.ts#L143-L151); `score-history-data.ts` consumes the same snapshot.
- **What's wrong:** `submit_grand_finale_prediction` decides which couples a late bracket can never earn points for using only `couples.status IN (eliminated, withdrawn, winner, runner_up, third_place)`. `couples.status` changes only when `commit_episode_publication` commits. Between an episode's East curtain and its publish, the couple going home that night is still 'active' and isn't snapshotted, so the late bracket is paid for that couple at publish (`weekly-score-rows.ts` drops only snapshotted couples). The commissioner's "Confirm that resolved couples will not be paid" guard reads the same lagging status, so they get no warning in that window either.
- **Why it matters:** two late unlocks are open and unused on live right now: manager 6686e0f3 in league f38df148 (`late_factor` 0.80) and manager cce0f95b in league 3e0e38cd (1.00). Neither has a bracket. Week 6 airs 2026-10-21 00:00 UTC. If either submits after the East broadcast and before publish, they can put the couple they just saw eliminated in the exact slot and collect the full payout. The window is real every week: weeks 3–5 published about 5 hours after `airs_at`, and week 2 aired 09-23 00:00 and published 09-28 21:23.
- **Fix:** stop relying on `couples.status` for the snapshot. Either (a) also mark as ineligible every couple in any episode with `airs_at <= now()` whose week isn't fully published, or (b) using the already-stored `submitted_at`, have `buildWeeklyScoreRows` treat a couple as ineligible for a late bracket when the first `airs_at` of its elimination week is before the unlock's `submitted_at`. Option (a) voids the late manager's points for every couple in that week, not only the eliminated one; that's probably acceptable since the outcome is public, but decide it deliberately. Use the same rule for the unlock confirmation so the commissioner is warned in that window.
- **Evidence:** code read of the lines above. Live read-only query of `grand_finale_late_unlocks` where `submitted_at` is null returned the two rows above, each with 0 `grand_finale_predictions`; air and publish times from the live `episodes` query. **1/1 upheld.** The verifier corrected the publish-lag figures (hours on weeks 3–5, days only on weeks 1–2) and flagged option (a)'s scope. Also in: SEC-7, BE-9.

**DATA-3 · In every live league, moving the Anchor Week reopens Grand Finale brackets after eliminations have aired; one live bracket was submitted after an elimination and was paid for it** — Medium
- **Where:** [schema.sql:1520](../supabase/schema.sql#L1519-L1520) (the `locking_exempt` skip in `update_scoring_categories`); [schema.sql:2749](../supabase/schema.sql#L2749) (`effective_grand_finale_deadline`, derived from the anchor); [schema.sql:3041](../supabase/schema.sql#L3041) (the deadline check in `submit_grand_finale_prediction`); `reset_draft` at [schema.sql:2343](../supabase/schema.sql#L2343); UI entry at [league-modules-form.tsx:1216](../src/components/league-modules-form.tsx#L1216-L1220), enabled because [settings/page.tsx:193](../src/app/leagues/[id]/settings/page.tsx#L193-L194) returns `scoringLocked = false` whenever `locking_exempt` is true; CLAUDE.md's Scoring and League Settings notes.
- **What's wrong:** all 4 drafted live leagues (f38df148, 3e0e38cd, d18784af, 8afa3b4f) have `scoring_settings.locking_exempt = true`, so `update_scoring_categories` skips the Season Clock lock and a commissioner can change `judges_score_starts_week` at any time. The Grand Finale deadline is derived from that anchor, and `submit_grand_finale_prediction` only checks `now()` against it. Moving the anchor to a future week reopens brackets for the whole league: anyone can delete and resubmit with known eliminations, with no late-unlock row and no ineligible snapshot, and the commissioner can then move the anchor back. A second path reopens brackets even when `locking_exempt` is false: `reset_draft` sets `draft_status = 'not_started'` with no deadline guard, and `effective_hard_deadline_week` then advances to the next unaired week. CLAUDE.md never mentions `locking_exempt` and says toggles and Reset to Neutral "stop once the Season Clock lock has passed", which is not true for any live league.
- **Why it matters:** manager c7ac8e17 in f38df148 submitted all 16 positions at 2026-09-23 02:53:48 UTC. The league's draft completed 09-22 04:18 with anchor 2, so the current deadline is 09-23 00:00, and the week-2 East broadcast had ended at 02:00 with couple eeda76ca eliminated. There is no late-unlock row. The bracket put eeda76ca at position 3 (exact), stored week-2 `grand_finale_points` = 8.33, × bonus weight 5 = 41.65 standings points. Current settings can't explain the row. Its cause is unproven: with no settings or schedule history, an anchor move, a later `airs_at` edit or a direct write would each explain it.
- **Fix:** keep the grandfathering (the `schema.sql` comment says it's deliberate), but stop any change from reopening brackets: stamp a `grand_finale_locked_at` on `scoring_settings` the first time the effective deadline passes, and have `submit_grand_finale_prediction` and the Grand Finale SELECT policy use it once set. This covers both the anchor and the `reset_draft` paths; rejecting anchor moves for exempt leagues would cover only the first. Document `locking_exempt` in CLAUDE.md.
- **Evidence:** PGlite copy of `schema.sql` with a repointable `auth.uid()`. With `locking_exempt = true`: the manager's resubmit after the deadline fails ("Grand Finale predictions are locked"); the commissioner moves the anchor 2→3, which succeeds; the manager's resubmit then succeeds with the eliminated couple moved to slot 1; the anchor goes back 3→2; late-unlock rows = 0. With `locking_exempt = false`, the anchor move fails ("Scoring settings are locked"). Live values from read-only SQL. **1/1 upheld.** The verifier added the `reset_draft` path, the UI entry point and the caveat on the live example's cause. Also in: SEC-5 (the `reset_draft` path); related: SEC-8.

**DATA-4 · Multi-night weeks: Grand Finale credit from earlier nights is dropped, and republishing night 1 pays survival to a couple eliminated on night 2** — Medium
- **Where:** [weekly-score-rows.ts:73](../src/lib/weekly-score-rows.ts#L59-L75); [scoring.ts:301](../src/lib/scoring.ts#L301); [score-history.ts:195](../src/lib/score-history.ts#L195-L197); [recompute-published-weeks.ts:84](../scripts/recompute-published-weeks.ts#L82-L88).
- **What's wrong:** every publish rewrites the whole week's `weekly_manager_scores`, and `buildWeeklyScoreRows` has two problems on weeks with more than one episode:
  - **(a)** It credits Grand Finale only for couples resolved by the episode being published (`newlyResolvedCoupleIds`). When night 2 publishes, the Grand Finale credit for night 1's eliminated couple is wiped and never restored.
  - **(b)** `rawOutcomeRows` puts other episodes' rows first and the published episode's rows last, and `computeWeeklyScores` keeps the last row per couple. So republishing night 1 lets its 'safe' row override a night-2 'eliminated' row and pays survival to an eliminated couple. A normal night-1-then-night-2 order is fine; it goes wrong only when the earlier night is the one being (re)published.
  - Score History orders rows by episode and credits every resolved couple, so it disagrees with stored totals in both cases. The recompute script picks whichever resolving row PostgREST returns first, which is arbitrary for S35 week 1.
- **Why it matters:** S35 week 1 was two nights with an elimination on each: e1da1e6f on E01 and 57ce5068 on E02, and E01 also stores a 'safe' row for 57ce5068. The pure engine on the live week-1 data, with an in-memory anchor of 1 and a manager who predicted both exactly, gives `grand_finale_points` 8.33 whether E01 or E02 is treated as current; the correct value is 16.66. With that manager holding 57ce5068, republishing E01 gives roster 4.99 vs 3.43 for E02 (+1.56 survival). No live impact today, because every drafted league's anchor is 2 and the recompute found 0 diffs. A league drafted before the premiere (like 4e2b3550, anchor 1) would be hit by the next two-night premiere. The survival half also needs night 1 to carry rows for night-2 couples; Enter Results narrows to participants when they're set (`episode-cast.ts`), but an unrestricted night or a correction seeded from S35 E01's 16 rows still produces them.
- **Fix:** compute `newlyResolvedCoupleIds` from every episode of the week (each couple's last resolving row by `episode_number`); Grand Finale ranges are already week-level. Order `rawOutcomeRows` by `episode_number`/`airs_at` instead of putting "this" episode last. Align the recompute script and add a two-night parity case to `score-history.test.ts`.
- **Evidence:** pure `buildWeeklyScoreRows` runs on the live dump with in-memory edits only: publish/republish E01 gave `roster_points` 4.99 and `grand_finale_points` 8.33; E02 gave 3.43 and 8.33. Live per-couple outcomes: 57ce5068 "1:safe 2:eliminated", e1da1e6f "1:eliminated". **1/1 upheld.** The verifier reproduced it separately and added that the reveal path wipes the same credit while night 2 is being revealed (DATA-10), and narrowed (b) to the earlier-night-republished order. Also in: BE-4.

**DATA-5 · A publish fails if anyone saves an unrelated pick in any league during it, even an unchanged re-save** — Medium
- **Where:** [schema.sql:3564](../supabase/schema.sql#L3561-L3566) (snapshot contents); [schema.sql:3609](../supabase/schema.sql#L3609) (the compare); [episode-publication.ts:176](../src/lib/episode-publication.ts#L171-L191) (two round trips); [weekly-score-rows.ts:127](../src/lib/weekly-score-rows.ts#L127-L129) (the week filter that shows most of the snapshot isn't an input); [schema.sql:2849](../supabase/schema.sql#L2849) (`submitted_at` bump).
- **What's wrong:** `episode_publication_snapshot` includes every row of `predictions`, `grand_finale_predictions`, `roster_slots`, `leagues` and `scoring_settings` across all leagues and weeks, and `commit_episode_publication` requires all of it to be identical between the snapshot read and the commit. Many of those rows aren't inputs to the week being published (`buildWeeklyScoreRows` drops other weeks' predictions). Still, any member saving next week's Curtain Call pick between the two RPCs fails the publish. So does re-saving an identical pick, because `submit_prediction` bumps `submitted_at`, and so does a draft pick in any league, because `leagues.current_turn_started_at` changes.
- **Why it matters:** publish happens right after the show, which is exactly when managers make next week's picks. The documented "compare, then refresh or retry" design is meant to catch changed inputs to this publication, not unrelated traffic. A scripted loop of `submit_prediction` calls for an open week, from any member of any league, keeps the window permanently dirty, and no row edit fixes that. The admin's only workaround is to retry until a gap opens.
- **Fix:** scope the snapshot to this week's real inputs: predictions where `week_id` is the episode's week, minus `submitted_at`; Grand Finale brackets and late unlocks only when the week resolves a couple; and only the scoring columns of `scoring_settings` and `leagues`, not draft or timer fields. Alternatively, take the locks first and build the snapshot inside the commit transaction.
- **Evidence:** PGlite test: took the snapshot and built the plan, then the victim league's manager called `submit_prediction` for next week (still open) as `authenticated`, then the commit ran: "Results or scoring inputs changed. Refresh and try again." An identical re-save of the same pick gave the same failure; a no-race control published OK. Nothing is locked until the commit ([schema.sql:3598](../supabase/schema.sql#L3598-L3606)). **1/1 upheld.** The verifier re-ran it with the control and corrected the line citations. Also in: OPS-20.

**DATA-6 · A Recast claim can back-date a couple into a week that is on air or already scored** — Medium
- **Where:** [schema.sql:3243](../supabase/schema.sql#L3243-L3254) (`submit_waiver_claim` computes `v_current_week`); [schema.sql:3155](../supabase/schema.sql#L3155-L3156) (`finalize_waiver_claim` starts at `week_number + 1`); `process_reverse_standings_waivers` at [schema.sql:3274](../supabase/schema.sql#L3274) and `approve_waiver_claim` at [schema.sql:3324](../supabase/schema.sql#L3324); `src/app/leagues/[id]/waivers/page.tsx` (no on-air gate).
- **What's wrong:** a claim is stamped with the latest week whose episodes are all 'completed', and the new couple starts the week after.
  - **During the show:** a week that has aired but isn't published doesn't count as completed, so a claim made during the broadcast, or while its scores are being live-revealed, starts the couple in that same week. In FCFS mode it's approved immediately.
  - **Delayed approval:** in manual or reverse-standings mode, `finalize_waiver_claim` reuses the stored week. A claim approved after the next week has published starts the couple in a week that has already been scored. Approval also doesn't re-check that the couple is still 'active', so a couple eliminated in the week that published between claim and approval can still be approved into the slot. (The slot itself is safe: finalize only updates the open row, and other pending claims for that slot are rejected.)
  - Neither path re-checks whether the start week has already aired.
- **Why it matters:** a manager with an eliminated couple can watch week N, or see a couple's 30 posted through per-couple Publish, claim it before official publish, and be paid its judges' points plus survival for week N. With delayed approval, Score History and the Couples Leaderboard re-derive from `roster_slots`, so they immediately show points for a past week that stored totals don't include, and the next republish of that week pays them. Every live league is `waiver_mode` 'locked', so neither path is exploitable today; it becomes live as soon as any league turns Recast on.
- **Fix:** compute the start week at finalize time instead of trusting the stored one: the first week whose first episode `airs_at` is still in the future, or reject claims between a week's first curtain and its publish. Inside `finalize_waiver_claim`, so all three resolution paths are covered, re-check that the couple is still active and not on a roster.
- **Evidence:** PGlite FCFS test: week 1 published, week 2 aired 90 minutes earlier with couple Y's 30 live-revealed, the manager's eliminated couple X in slot 1. `submit_waiver_claim` returned approved with `week_number` 1; `roster_slots` became X 1–1 and Y from week 2; `buildWeeklyScoreRows` for week 2 gave that manager `roster_points` 5.82 (30 × 0.1421 + 1.56 survival). PGlite manual test: claimed before week 2 aired (pending, week 1), week 2 published, then the commissioner's approve returned approved with Y starting week 2. Live read-only: `waiver_mode` 'locked' on all 5 leagues and 0 `waiver_claims` rows. **1/1 upheld.** The verifier added the missing active-couple re-check at approval. Also in: BE-10, BE-22.

**DATA-7 · A commissioner can reopen this week's Curtain Call after the elimination airs by switching the coast, then switch back with no record of the change** — Medium
- **Where:** [schema.sql:1433](../supabase/schema.sql#L1433-L1434) (unguarded writes inside `update_league_settings`, 1404–1446); [schema.sql:2667](../supabase/schema.sql#L2667) (`prediction_lock_at`, recomputed from current values on every call); [schema.sql:2831](../supabase/schema.sql#L2829-L2833) (the lock check in `submit_prediction`).
- **What's wrong:** `update_league_settings` writes `prediction_lock_coast` and `prediction_lock_hours_before_air` at any time, with no check against weeks that are on air or locked. After the East broadcast has shown the elimination, an East league's commissioner can switch to west (the lock moves to 8pm PT minus hours, capped at the West broadcast end), submit the known elimination (as can any member who acts in that window), and switch back. `submit_prediction` accepts the pick. While the league is switched, the peer-pick RLS policy also re-hides picks that were already visible after the East lock.
- **Why it matters:** for an 8pm ET episode (2026-10-21 00:00Z, 120 minutes), the East broadcast ends at 02:00Z:

  | Setting | Lock | Relative to the East broadcast end |
  |---|---|---|
  | east, 0 | 00:00Z | locked 2h before it ends |
  | west, 0 | 03:00Z | 1h after |
  | west, −2 | 05:00Z | 3h after (the cap) |

  That's up to 3 hours to pick an elimination already shown on East, for a full exact Curtain Call payout. Afterwards the league looks East-locked again. `predictions.submitted_at` still shows the pick came after the restored lock, but `leagues` has no `updated_at` or `updated_by`, so the settings change itself leaves no record. The weeks 4–5 late picks from manager 5b873ac9 in East league 8afa3b4f (DATA-8) are consistent with this path: they have microsecond-precision, single-league timestamps, which is what `now()` inside the RPC produces. That is consistency, not proof.
- **Fix:** freeze a week's lock once it's reached: in `update_league_settings`, reject any coast or hours change that would move an already-passed lock later for an unpublished week, or store the computed lock per (league, week) the first time it passes. Add `updated_at`/`updated_by` to settings changes as cheap hardening on top.
